# Transition Pattern Library

Select one primary grammar that follows the source content. Do not combine every pattern.

## Water, Sea, Lake, Sky

- Use uneven waterlines, layered ripples, foam contours, horizontal scan bands, or pigment washes.
- Continue real water texture downward as halftone lines; let designed ripple lines rise into the photographed water.
- Preserve visible swimmers, boats, buoys, reflections, and their counts as readable symbols below.

## Landscape, Trees, Mountains

- Use torn paper fibers, topographic contours, leaf silhouettes, erosion edges, or translucent terrain washes.
- Align designed contours with the photographed skyline or vegetation boundary.
- Let one real branch, ridge, or stone fragment cross the edge when composition permits.

## Architecture, Streets, Rides

- Use structural grids, construction lines, perforated edges, facade modules, map fragments, or geometric cutouts.
- Continue real columns, spokes, cables, windows, rails, or road lines into the design zone.
- Preserve dominant building order and relative scale in simplified linework.

## People And Prominent Objects

- Use a clipped photographic contour that protrudes across the edge, then dissolve it into silhouette, linework, halftone, or paper cutout.
- Keep identity only in the photographic fragment; use faceless non-realistic symbols in the design area.
- Preserve subject count, relative size, pose direction, and interaction.

## Motion

- Use interrupted trajectories, scanline drift, sequential fragments, registration offset, or a single motion trace.
- Align the trace to a real movement direction from the source.

## Tickets, Documents, Printed Matter

- Use torn stub edges, perforation notches, staggered paper layers, dashed cut lines, stamp texture, or xerox halftone.
- Let one real document or device protrude across the tear; let derived outlines rise back into the photo.
- Replace sensitive or identifying text with illegible microtexture. Never invent plausible personal data.

## Prompt Skeleton

```text
Tall vertical 3:5 poster on warm ivory fibrous paper. Keep the clear original photograph in the upper 43%-48%, with narrow top and side borders and no straight rectangular bottom edge. Preserve [invariants].

Build a 10%-15% bidirectional transition using [primary grammar] plus [support texture]. Extend [photo feature] downward 3%-8%; extend [design feature] upward 2%-5% and align it with [real source feature]. Carry [accent hue], [continuous contour], and [shared texture] across the boundary. No clean divider or empty separator.

Below, translate the source into [one readable relation], preserving [count / order / scale / interaction]. Use [anchor form], tiny [typography] for "[short title]", and [accent hue] as the main color anchor.

Flat scanned-paper appearance, diffuse light, low-to-medium contrast, [mood]. Avoid straight split, isolated panels, unrelated torn paper, duplicate subjects, commercial styling, glossy mockup, 3D, neon, full-bleed illustration, and dense collage.
```

## Quality Questions

- Does the transition arise from the photograph's own subject or structure?
- Do photo fragments visibly move down and design elements visibly move up?
- Is there a continuous line, color, and texture crossing the boundary?
- Can the lower graphic still be recognized at thumbnail size?
- Is the original subject preserved without realistic duplication?
